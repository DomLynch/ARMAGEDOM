## Idle playback (what OpenTrident did not verify)
- `Web/tests/hub-npcs.test.js` plays each NPC's `Idle` through the engine's own `DonorMotion` at 11 phases, asserts head above hips above both feet (by more than 0.3 m), feet on the ground (ankle y between -0.05 and 0.25 m), finite matrices, head and hands move at least 1 cm, spine stays within 10 cm, and the loop closes (end within 25 cm of start). It also checks bytes, sha256 and the OpenTrident record, one `npc/` folder, 106 joints, at most 16 draws, no weapon nodes, size under 600 KB.
- `shoot.mjs` + `idle.html` repeat the check in headless Chromium (Apple GPU, muted) on the game's own central-hub camera rig, lights and no tone mapping, and take the stills. Result for all three: Idle plays, upright, pose changes (head range about 3 cm, hands about 50 cm), feet 0.06 to 0.09 m off the origin plane (the ankle height, same as the crowd bodies).
- **The engine does not animate a standing human's idle.** `DonorMotion.update()` for a stationary entity samples `Idle` at phase 0 on every tick (a held pose; the test asserts this). The NPCs breathe and shift only if the Hub wiring PR drives `sample(idle, phase)` itself, the way `engine/remote-survivors.js` offsets its idle clock. Crowd residents have the same behaviour today.
- The `Idle` clip is the same 4 s Mixamo clip as on the crowd bodies (measured: identical foot, head and hip numbers on `mh-man-01`). It is a loose, forward-leaning stance with a wide foot gap (about 0.5 to 0.6 m), the head tipped down and a look over the shoulder around phase 0.6; from the high game camera it reads as a slouch, not a posed greeter. A calmer per-NPC idle is an art follow-up, not done here.
