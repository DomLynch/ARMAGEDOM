# Crow: a big, ragged, glowing-eyed carrion crow on the rat rig (28 Oct 2026)

Authority: Strategy ruled GO on six new monsters (28 Oct); this is the crow. Nothing generated by a model, nothing downloaded, no paid service: every shape and texture is built by script.

Pipeline, all CPU (VPS `/srv/dev-jobs/art-crow/`): `run_crow.sh` runs `atlas_crow.py` (Pillow + numpy), `build_crow.py` (Blender 5.2.2 headless), `gltf-transform meshopt --level medium`, then `anim_cmp.py`.
- Source: `Web/public/assets/rat/original-rat.glb` (the game's own rat: its 24 bones, 7 clips, scene graph and mesh name). Nothing else is imported.
- Rig kept exactly: armature `ARM_RatRig`, the same 24 joints and names (`anim_cmp.py`: joints equal True), the same seven clips `rat_idle/walk/run/hit/death/bite/bite_low` with the source durations (0.6, 0.9, 0.333, 1.6, 0.5, 0.8, 0.6 s). The rat clips are 30 fps, so `build_crow.py` sets the scene to 30 fps before import (the roach fix, f265b49b); at the factory 24 fps every clip lost its last frame.
- Mesh: replaced completely. Hind legs become the crow's legs and feet (thigh feathers, tarsus, three toes with claws and a hind toe), the front legs become half-open, dropped wings (flight feathers and two rows of coverts, a few broken quills, split tips), the tail chain carries a nine-feather ragged tail, head and jaw carry a heavy hooked bill and a thinner lower bill, plus hackles and crown tufts. The bird stays on the ground: wings half open, no flight system.
- Skin weights: no copying from the rat. Each vertex is weighted from its position along the existing bones (body and neck by distance along the spine, wings to `chest` + `leg_F*` + `shin_F*`, legs by height to `leg_B*`, `shin_B*`, `paw_B*`, tail by distance to `tail_1..3`, bill to `head`, `jaw`, `bite_tip`). No new bones, no re-parenting; at most four influences per vertex.
- Walk and run swing the front-leg bones, so the wings flick forward and back while the bird moves, and the bite lunges the head and snaps the bill, with no new clip.
- Mesh and material layout matches the rat: one mesh `Original ARM rat surface` with four primitives in rat order (feathers, legs, eyes, bill). Why: the shared rat bite code reads vertex 80 of primitive four (`Original_ARM_rat_surface_3`, actors.js `tooth()`, `toothVertex` 80 in appearance.json) as the biting point. The bill primitive has 114 vertices, so vertex 80 exists and lies on the bill (rest position about 0.19 m forward of the origin in model space, pinned by `Web/tests/crow-monster.test.js`).
- Materials: `Glossy black carrion feathers` (the coat; textured, guarded by rig row `coatName`), `Scaled ash-grey legs and feet`, `Pale glowing eyes` (emissive), `Dark horn beak`.
- Textures (WebP, in the GLB): albedo 1024x512, ORM 512x256, normal 512x256. The left square is a scallop plumage tile for the body, the right square a single flight feather (shaft, barbs, frayed tips); both carry a baked oil-sheen (blue, teal, violet) on a dark brown-black base, glossy in the roughness channel; the normal map is a Sobel of the same height field.
- Budget: see the numbers in the PR body (triangles under 8k, GLB under 600 KB, textures at most 1024).

Game wiring is all data (no game code beyond two small reads): see the PR body.

Reproduce: copy `original-rat.glb` into `/srv/dev-jobs/art-crow/`, then `bash run_crow.sh`; the result is `crow.glb` (not byte-stable across Blender builds, so the sha256 is pinned in `manifest-hollow.json`).

Stills: `stills/` (375 px wide, in game and in the model viewer, `viewer.mjs`).
