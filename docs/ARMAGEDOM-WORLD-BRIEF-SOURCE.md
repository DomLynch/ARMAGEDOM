Use this as the **core narrative + world brief for the dev team**:

## ARMAGEDOM — Story & World Brief

**Genre:** Post-apocalyptic action RPG  
**Perspective:** Close elevated/isometric 3D — Diablo / Path of Exile style, but with larger, more visible characters and more physical combat  
**Tone:** Fallout + Mad Max + Metro, with a darker near-future realism  
**Setting:** Approximately **2045**, around 15 years after civilisation collapsed

### Premise

In **2029–2030**, the world enters a cascading global crisis.

Regional wars spread. Energy and food shortages worsen. Cyberattacks cripple infrastructure. Governments become increasingly unstable. A series of military miscalculations eventually leads to a **limited nuclear exchange**.

It does not completely destroy humanity.

It destroys the system that kept humanity functioning.

Power grids collapse. Supply chains disappear. Governments fragment. Major cities become contaminated or abandoned. Billions die over the following years from war, famine, disease and social collapse.

Fifteen years later, humanity has reorganised into hundreds of small territories.

There is **no single government, no reliable law and very little advanced manufacturing**.

Old highways connect fortified settlements. Abandoned cities contain valuable technology but also radiation, mutants, automated military systems and hostile scavengers.

Fuel, ammunition, clean water and functioning technology are more valuable than money.

This world is known simply as:

# **ARMAGEDOM**

---

## Player fantasy

You are **not the chosen one**.

You begin as an unknown survivor with basic clothes, a damaged weapon and almost nothing else.

You survive by:

**Fight → scavenge → loot → upgrade → explore → trade → choose allies → become powerful.**

Over time the player can become a feared mercenary, scavenger, warlord, trader, explorer or faction leader.

The world should feel dangerous initially and gradually become conquerable as the player gains equipment, knowledge and reputation.

---

## World style

Avoid futuristic glossy sci-fi.

Everything should look **used, repaired, scavenged and believable**.

Think:

- ruined highways
- collapsed cities
- desert settlements
- abandoned shopping malls
- underground bunkers
- military installations
- destroyed factories
- oil refineries
- flooded tunnels
- contaminated forests
- vehicle graveyards
- improvised settlements built from shipping containers and wreckage

Technology is a mixture of:

**2020s technology + military equipment + improvised engineering + recovered experimental technology.**

A rifle held together with tape and scavenged parts should make as much sense as a rare functioning military drone.

---

## Combat

Combat should feel **heavy, readable and physical**.

Do NOT make it an Asian-MMO-style fireworks display.

Weapons include:

**Melee:** machetes, axes, pipes, knives, hammers, chainsaws, improvised weapons.

**Firearms:** pistols, shotguns, rifles, SMGs, sniper rifles.

**Improvised:** Molotovs, mines, tripwires, grenades, traps.

**Rare technology:** drones, powered armour, experimental energy weapons, implants.

The equivalent of fantasy "magic" comes from:

**technology, mutations, drugs, cybernetics and experimental weapons.**

Example:

Fantasy fireball → incendiary grenade  
Mage shield → portable energy shield  
Necromancer summon → combat drone  
Poison spell → chemical weapon  
Teleport → short-range experimental mobility device  
Magic buffs → stimulants / implants

This allows ARPG build diversity **without traditional magic**.

---

## Enemy families

Start grounded.

**Raiders** — humans with improvised weapons and armour.

**Scavengers** — organised hostile groups searching ruins.

**Mutants** — humans/animals altered by radiation, chemicals or biological experimentation.

**Machines** — abandoned military drones, robots and defence systems.

**Faction soldiers** — organised military groups with better equipment.

Later enemies can become stranger, but the world should initially feel plausible rather than supernatural.

---

## Major factions

Keep approximately **4–6 strong factions**, not dozens.

Example concepts:

**The Authority**  
Remnants of military/government forces. Order, discipline and technology—but increasingly authoritarian.

**The Free Settlements**  
Independent towns trying to rebuild normal civilisation.

**The Reavers**  
Nomadic raiders controlling highways, fuel and vehicles.

**The Vaultborn**  
People who survived in underground facilities and possess superior technology but little understanding of the outside world.

**The Changed**  
Radiation-affected humans who have begun forming their own civilisation rather than seeing themselves as victims.

**The Machine Cult**  
People who believe surviving AI and pre-war machines should guide humanity's future.

No faction should simply be "good guys versus bad guys."

---

## Central story

Rumours spread that beneath the ruins of an old military research complex exists **EDEN**—a pre-collapse system capable of restoring power, water purification and automated manufacturing across an enormous region.

Every major faction wants it.

But EDEN may also contain the technology responsible for escalating the original catastrophe.

The player gradually discovers that controlling EDEN could:

**rebuild civilisation, create an empire, or trigger another catastrophe.**

The main storyline becomes a race between factions to find and control it.

This gives us a strong long-term narrative without making the player the centre of the universe from minute one.

# MVP / First Playable Area

For the first version, **do not build the whole lore**.

Build one small area that communicates the entire fantasy.

### Location

A ruined desert highway leading into an abandoned roadside settlement and underground bunker.

### Player starts with

- improvised melee weapon
- old pistol
- basic clothing
- dodge
- simple grenade

### Enemies

1. Basic raider
2. Heavy raider
3. Rifleman
4. Mutated animal
5. Boss — heavily armoured raider leader

### Gameplay

Player enters settlement → fights raiders → searches buildings → finds better gun/armour → discovers bunker entrance → fights mutants/machines underground → boss encounter → finds first clue about **EDEN**.

Target playtime:

**10–20 minutes.**

The player should finish wanting to know:

> **What else is out there?**

---

## Design principle

**Do not build Fallout with different names.**

Armagedom should become:

> **A fast, physical isometric ARPG set in a believable collapsed near-future world, combining Diablo-style progression with Mad Max-style survival, scavenging and faction conflict.**

The differentiator is the combination of:

**ARPG combat + meaningful loot + post-apocalyptic survival + faction/world consequences + readable physical combat.**

For the MVP, prioritise **combat feel, atmosphere and loot progression**. Everything else can come later.
